TDR Painter

Paint an impedance profile, drop in parasitics, and watch return loss, insertion loss and ILD respond. Every cell of the profile is a short lossy line; the frequency views are the exact cascade.

Units

Lengths, positions, stubs and the brush in inches and mil, or millimeters; conductor loss and DC resistance per inch or per centimeter. Saved with the project; the file itself always stores inches.

Experimental

Tx and Rx, the painted profile, stub impedance and series R go from 1 mΩ (a short) to 1 MΩ (an open), and Rx gets Short and Open buttons. Saved with the project. Turning it off brings values back into the normal ranges; Undo restores them.

How the model works
  • The painted profile is 500 equal cells. Each cell is a uniform line of its painted impedance; lumped elements sit on cell boundaries. S-parameters come from the ABCD cascade, so they are exact for this structure.
  • R′ and L′ scale with Z and C′ with 1/Z, as if width alone changed the impedance. Loss per inch is the same everywhere, and only the reflections change with what you paint.
  • Dielectric: ε(f) = ε∞ + Δε·ln((f₂+jf)/(f₁+jf)) / ln(f₂/f₁), anchored to Dk and Df at f_ref. Conductor: R_dc + (1+j)·K·√f.
  • TDR: S11 times a Kaiser half-window, inverse FFT, trapezoid-rule integration, then Z = Z_Tx(1+ρ)/(1−ρ). The window sets rise time and ringing; the orange trace is what a TDR would show, the indigo line is the truth.
  • With loss on, the orange trace sits a little above the painted line and creeps upward along it. Skin-effect resistance and internal inductance raise the low-frequency impedance. Tick Lossless conductor to see it vanish.
  • Labels on short painted bumps use the TDR area method: L = 2Z₀∫ρ dt, C = −(2/Z₀)∫ρ dt over round-trip time. Long excursions show average impedance and length instead.
  • ILD is dB(S21) minus a least-squares fit of a0 + a1√f + a2·f + a4·f² over the whole sweep.
  • Privacy: the page makes no network requests and keeps nothing, not even in browser storage. Save writes a .tdrp file to your computer (a ZIP holding project.json, with a CRC-32 that Open verifies) and Open reads it back; Export JSON writes the same project as readable text. The standalone build adds a Content-Security-Policy that blocks every outgoing connection, so the browser itself enforces this.
  • Attenuator: an ideal resistive T pad with image impedances Zin (Tx side) and Zout (Rx side). With K = 10^(dB/10), the shunt resistor is 2√(K·Zin·Zout)/(K−1) and each series resistor Z(K+1)/(K−1) minus it. When Zin ≠ Zout the loss can't go below the minimum-loss pad, 20·log₁₀(√r + √(r−1)) with r the impedance ratio.
  • Nothing radiates and nothing couples. Power that is neither reflected nor transmitted is absorbed in the line or in series R.
  • Experimental impedance range (gear menu): 1 mΩ is a short and 1 MΩ an open, for the ports, the profile, stubs and series R. The S-parameters stay referenced to Tx and Rx, so with Rx shorted S21 is tiny by definition; the TDR is the useful view. A painted 1 mΩ section keeps the same loss per inch as any other cell. Stubs can be open or shorted at the far end: a 1 mΩ shorted stub is a short to ground at its position, DC included, while a 1 mΩ open stub is a nanofarad capacitor that is open at DC.
